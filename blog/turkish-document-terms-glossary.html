Turkish Document Terms

The words you will meet on documents — from TAC Notary Public Istanbul

Turkish offices, forms and certificates use words that don't translate neatly, and a single mix-up can mean ordering the wrong document. This glossary explains the terms we hear most often from foreigners.

Documents and records

TurkishEnglishWhat it is
Nüfus MüdürlüğüCivil registry officeThe office that issues civil status documents
Nüfus Kayıt ÖrneğiFamily registry recordA Turkish-language record of civil registry details. The full version (vukuatlı) includes all family members
Formül A / B / CMultilingual extractsInternational extracts for birth (A), marriage (B) and death (C), issued in Turkish plus another language
Aile CüzdanıFamily bookletThe booklet that records a marriage and the children
Adli Sicil KaydıCriminal record certificateA certificate showing criminal record status
İkamet İzniResidence permitPermission for a foreigner to live in Turkey
Vergi NumarasıTax numberThe tax identification number. See Tax Number in Turkey for Foreigners

Notary documents

TurkishEnglishWhat it is
NoterNotaryThe official who confirms identity and signatures
VekaletnamePower of attorneyAuthorizes another person to act for you. See Power of Attorney in Turkey for Foreigners
MuvafakatnameConsent letterWritten permission, for example for a child to travel. See Affidavit and Consent Letter in Turkey

Translation and certification

TurkishEnglishWhat it is
Yeminli TercümeSworn translationA translation by a sworn translator. See Sworn vs. Notarized Translation
Noter Onaylı TercümeNotarized translationA translation certified by a notary
ApostilApostilleThe certification for use in Hague Convention countries. See What Is an Apostille?
Valilik / KaymakamlıkGovernorship / District governorshipThe offices that issue apostilles in Turkey
e-DevletE-Government portalThe online government portal where many documents are ordered

Why the terms matter

  • Similar names, different documents. A multilingual extract and a family registry record are not interchangeable. See Turkish Birth and Marriage Certificates for Use Abroad.
  • Sworn is not the same as notarized. A document may need both, and the receiving office decides.
  • Always use the exact name. When you ask a Turkish office for a document, use its Turkish name, so you receive the right one.
