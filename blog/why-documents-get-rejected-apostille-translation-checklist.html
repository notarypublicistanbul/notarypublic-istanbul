Why Documents Get Rejected

A checklist to run before you submit — from TAC Notary Public Istanbul

Most rejected documents are not rejected for anything complicated. The same few mistakes come up again and again with apostilles, translations and notarized documents. Run through this list before you submit anything, and you avoid most of them.

1. Ask the receiving office first

The office that receives your document decides what it accepts: which document type, whether it wants an apostille or a legalization, whether it needs a translation, and sometimes how recent the document must be. Ask it directly, and keep the answer in writing if you can. Everything below is easier once you know what it wants.

2. Use the right certification route

If the destination country belongs to the Hague Apostille Convention, the document needs an apostille. If it doesn't, an apostille will not be recognized and the document needs consular legalization instead. An apostille on a document going to a non-member country is one of the most expensive mistakes, because the time and fees are lost. See Apostille or Consular Legalization? for how to tell the two apart.

3. Do the steps in the right order

DirectionOrder
Turkish document for use abroadSworn translation, notarization of the translation, then the apostille (or legalization)
Foreign document for use in TurkeyApostille or legalization in the country that issued it, then sworn translation into Turkish and notarization in Turkey

For foreign documents, the certification has to happen in the issuing country, so arrange it before you travel. Our guide to foreign documents for use in Turkey goes through it in detail.

4. Check names, dates and spellings

A name spelled differently from your passport is a frequent cause of rejection, and a translation repeats whatever spelling the original document has. Compare names, dates of birth and place names across your passport, your certificates and any forms before you start, so any mismatch is fixed at the source and not discovered at the counter.

5. Request the right document

Many countries issue more than one document with a similar name, and the receiving office usually wants a specific one. Turkish civil registry documents are a common example, where a multilingual extract and a family registry record serve different purposes. We cover this in Turkish Birth and Marriage Certificates for Use Abroad. Ordering the wrong one wastes weeks.

6. Match the wording and details they expect

For affidavits and consent letters, the text itself matters. Ask the person or office abroad for their draft, so that the wording and details fit what they expect, and check who has to sign and whether the document has to be recent. We explain this in Affidavit and Consent Letter in Turkey.

7. Don't add steps nobody requires

Extra steps cost money and delay without making a document stronger. For a Hague destination, a Turkish document needs a sworn translation, its notarization and the apostille, and nothing more. If someone tells you a step you have not heard of is needed, ask who requires it.

The checklist

  • I know exactly which document the receiving office wants.
  • I know whether the destination needs an apostille or consular legalization.
  • The steps are in the right order for the direction the document is travelling.
  • Names, dates and spellings match my passport.
  • For an affidavit or consent letter, I have the receiving office's draft and I know who must sign.
