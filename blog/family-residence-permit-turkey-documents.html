Family Residence Permit in Turkey

Which documents need an apostille and translation — from TAC Notary Public Istanbul

A family residence permit lets the spouse and children of a foreign resident (or of a Turkish citizen) live in Turkey. The application is filed online, but it falls apart on the paperwork: foreign certificates that haven't been apostilled, or translated the wrong way. Here is how to get that part right.

The documents that need extra steps

  • Marriage certificate (if you are applying as a spouse). It must carry an apostille from the country that issued it, then be translated into Turkish and the translation notarized.
  • Birth certificate (for each child). The same rule: apostille first, then a notarized Turkish translation.
  • A court ruling, if the sponsoring parent is divorced and is the child's legal guardian. A notarized Turkish translation of the ruling is generally required.

What doesn't need the full treatment

  • Passport photocopies. Universities and migration offices commonly state that no translation or approval is needed for photocopies of the passport.
  • Documents issued by Turkish authorities. These should be e-signed or signed and stamped by the issuing Turkish office. They don't need an apostille.

The order that works

StepWhere
1. Get a recent original certificateThe country that issued it
2. ApostilleThe issuing country's apostille authority
3. Turkish translation by a sworn translatorTurkey
4. Notarization of the translationA Turkish notary
5. Submit with the online (e-ikamet) applicationYour provincial migration office

If your country isn't in the Apostille Convention

An apostille only works between member countries. If your country isn't one, the documents usually need to be approved by that country's authorities, and by the Turkish consulate or the foreign ministry, before they are translated. Ask early, because this takes longer.

Mistakes that cause deficiency notices

  • Translating before the apostille. The apostille belongs on the original certificate, and the translation (with the apostille translated too) follows.
  • Skipping the notary. An ordinary translation, even by a good translator, isn't enough. The translation has to be notarized in Turkey.
  • Name or date mismatches. Spellings that differ from the passport, or errors introduced in translation, are a common reason a file is sent back.
  • Old documents. Some offices want a recently issued certificate. Check with your provincial migration office before ordering one.
  • Assuming every office is identical. Rules are national, but each provincial directorate can ask for small extras. Look at what your own office asks for.
