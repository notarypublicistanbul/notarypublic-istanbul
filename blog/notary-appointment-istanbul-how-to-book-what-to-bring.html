Notes from the Office

Notary Appointment in Istanbul

How to book, and how to prepare — from TAC Notary Public Istanbul

Some documents can be handled without leaving home. Others have to be signed in person before a notary. If yours is one of them, a little preparation turns the visit into a short, uneventful one.

What has to be signed in person

Powers of attorney, affidavits and consent letters are signed by the person making them, in front of the notary. They cannot be done online. Read more in our guides to power of attorney in Turkey for foreigners and affidavits and consent letters.

Other services can run remotely. For documents that qualify, our E-Apostille service works without a visit.

How to book

Office visits are by appointment only. Message us through the website, by email or on WhatsApp. We don't take phone calls and we can't see walk-ins, so please arrange your time with us first. Appointments are during working hours, 9am to 5pm.

When you message us, tell us three things so we can plan the visit properly: what the document is, which country or office it is for, and whether it needs a translation or an apostille afterwards.

What to bring

  • Your identity document, with your name spelled as it appears on your passport.
  • Your Turkish tax number or residence permit, if you have one. If you don't have a tax number, we take care of getting it issued for you.
  • The draft, if the receiving office or the person abroad gave you one. Send it to us before the appointment so the text can be prepared in advance.
  • The details the document needs. For a consent letter, that means the child's passport details, the destination, the travel dates and the name of the accompanying adult. For a power of attorney, the details of the person you are authorizing and exactly what they may do.
  • Any documents that need translation or an apostille, if they are part of the same job.

What happens after the signing

If the document is going abroad and the receiving office needs it in another language, the route is a sworn translation, notarization of the translation, and then the apostille, or legalization for non-member countries. We handle those steps for you. See Apostille or Consular Legalization? if you aren't sure which one applies to your destination.

Mistakes that cost time

  • Arriving without an appointment. Visits are by appointment only.
  • Name spellings that don't match the passport. They cause problems later, especially after translation.
  • Not having the draft. If the receiving office has wording it expects, get it before you come.
  • Not knowing the destination. The country decides whether the document needs an apostille or legalization, and whether it needs translating.
  • Not checking who has to sign. If more than one person is involved, confirm who needs to attend before booking.

Ready to book? Tell us the document, the country and the purpose.
